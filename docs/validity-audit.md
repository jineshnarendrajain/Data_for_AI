# Validity Audit

## Project

Manhattan Urban Heat Hotspot Prioritisation and Heat Risk Prediction

---

## Purpose

This audit evaluates the validity of the conclusions generated by the urban heat analysis and prediction workflow.

The objective is to assess whether the evidence supports the conclusions drawn and whether the analytical framework and trained model are appropriate for decision-support purposes.

---

# Validity Question 1

## Does the analysis measure what it claims to measure?

### Claim

The workflow identifies relative urban heat hotspots across Manhattan.

### Evidence

The workflow combines:

* Land Surface Temperature (LST)
* Vegetation Density (NDVI)

using:

```text
Heat Risk Score =
Normalized LST − Normalized NDVI
```

### Assessment

LST directly measures surface thermal conditions.

NDVI provides a widely used proxy for vegetation density.

Both indicators are relevant to urban heat island analysis.

### Verdict

```text
VALID
```

# Threats to Validity

## Threat 1

### Temporal Limitation

The analysis only represents Summer 2018 conditions.

Potential Impact:

Results may differ in other years.

Severity:

Medium

---

## Threat 2

### No External Validation

The workflow has not yet been compared against:

```text
NYC Heat Vulnerability Index (HVI)
```

Potential Impact:

Independent confirmation is currently unavailable.

Severity:

Medium

---

## Threat 3

### Relative Risk Interpretation

The heat risk score is not a direct measurement of:

* Human thermal comfort
* Mortality risk
* Medical heat stress

Potential Impact:

Outputs could be misinterpreted if limitations are ignored.

Severity:

Low

---

## Threat 4

### Framework-Derived Training Data

The model was trained on data generated from the heat-risk framework itself.

Potential Impact:

High predictive performance demonstrates successful reproduction of the framework but does not independently validate the framework against external observations.

Severity:

Medium

# Conclusion

The evidence supports the conclusion that the workflow can identify relative urban heat hotspots across Manhattan and that the trained model can accurately reproduce the resulting heat-risk framework.

The analytical workflow and Heat Risk Prediction Model are suitable for exploratory planning, educational use, and future decision-support applications, provided that documented limitations are acknowledged.

Overall validity status:

```text
PASS WITH DOCUMENTED LIMITATIONS
```
