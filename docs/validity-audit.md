# Validity Audit

## Project

Manhattan Urban Heat Hotspot Prioritisation

---

## Purpose

This audit evaluates the validity of the conclusions generated by the urban heat hotspot analysis workflow.

The objective is to assess whether the evidence supports the conclusions drawn and whether the outputs are appropriate for decision-support purposes.

---

# Validity Question 1

## Does the analysis measure what it claims to measure?

### Claim

The workflow identifies relative urban heat hotspots across Manhattan.

### Evidence

The workflow combines:

* Land Surface Temperature (LST)
* Vegetation Density (NDVI)

using:

```text
Heat Risk Score =
Normalized LST − Normalized NDVI
```

### Assessment

LST directly measures surface thermal conditions.

NDVI provides a widely used proxy for vegetation density.

Both indicators are relevant to urban heat island analysis.

### Verdict

```text
VALID
```

# Validity Question 3

## Are the conclusions supported by observed variation?

### Evidence

#### LST

| Metric | Value    |
| ------ | -------- |
| Min    | 10.33 °C |
| Mean   | 30.73 °C |
| Max    | 49.20 °C |

#### NDVI

| Metric | Value |
| ------ | ----- |
| Min    | -0.27 |
| Mean   | 0.09  |
| Max    | 0.70  |

### Assessment

Both datasets show substantial variation across the study area.

This variation allows meaningful differentiation between lower-risk and higher-risk locations.

### Verdict

```text
VALID
```

---

# Validity Question 4

## Are hotspot outputs plausible?

### Evidence

```text
Top hotspot samples extracted: 10
```

### Assessment

The workflow successfully identified a limited number of higher-risk locations rather than classifying the entire study area uniformly.

This behaviour is consistent with expected urban heat island patterns.

### Verdict

```text
VALID
```


---

# Threats to Validity

## Threat 1

### Temporal Limitation

The analysis only represents Summer 2018 conditions.

Potential Impact:

Results may differ in other years.

Severity:

Medium

---

## Threat 2

### No External Validation

The workflow has not yet been compared against:

```text
NYC Heat Vulnerability Index (HVI)
```

Potential Impact:

Independent confirmation is currently unavailable.

Severity:

Medium

---

## Threat 3

### Relative Risk Interpretation

The heat risk score is not a direct measurement of:

* human thermal comfort
* mortality risk
* medical heat stress

Potential Impact:

Outputs could be misinterpreted if limitations are ignored.

Severity:

Low

# Conclusion

The evidence supports the conclusion that the workflow can identify relative urban heat hotspots across Manhattan.

The analysis is suitable for exploratory planning, educational use, and decision-support applications, provided that its limitations are acknowledged.

Overall validity status:

```text
PASS WITH DOCUMENTED LIMITATIONS
```
